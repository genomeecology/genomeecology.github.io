Toby is a group leader and DDLS fellow at the Swedish Museum of Natural History. He has a broad range of interests spanning transposable elements (TEs), genomics from populations to entire kingdoms, and the development of tools to facilitate genomic research. His research focuses on understanding the mechanisms that drive genome innovation and host adaptation across the tree of life, with a particular emphasis on the role of mobile DNA in shaping genome structure, adaptation, and biodiversity over deep evolutionary time. Toby’s major aims include leveraging emerging technologies, such an interpretable AI, to explore the diversity of mobile DNA across the tree of life, developing computational tools to analyze genomic data, and investigating the evolutionary dynamics of TEs in various organisms.

Outside the lab, Toby spends most of his time in the outdoors: climbing, trail running, cycling, skiing, wild swimming, or just wandering in the woods. He’s a firm believer in Type 2 fun as a healthy counterweight to life behind a screen. This inevitably comes with an ever-growing interest in outdoor gear and an unhealthy amount of time spent researching kit he probably doesn’t need (he’s also an advocate for the N+1 bikes rule…). The one indoor pursuit that competes: third-wave speciality coffee. He’ll happily make the case for a well-pulled espresso or slow pour-over to anyone within earshot.
